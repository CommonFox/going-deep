The plan

Rounds 1–5: 2 RB · 2 WR · 1 TE. QB is flexible — take one in the mix if the value is there, never forced.

This composition (or a close 1QB-in-the-mix variant) is #1 at every one of the 10 possible seats — 300 simulated drafts each — and lands in the top statistically-supported cluster of the league-wide 81,000-draft field study too. Unlike the superflex league, there's no QB run to chase: this league starts one QB, so a second carries no scarcity premium.

Why QB is different here

QBs still never bust — the same 0% bust rate across every archetype the Sleeper sheet found. What's different is the payoff: one QB slot means a second quarterback earns no scarcity premium, so unlike the superflex league there's no forced QB run. Take Allen if he somehow falls; otherwise the whole T2/T3 QB group is close enough in value that waiting costs little.

Endgame notes

K, P and DST are held until round 18. This league starts three "late" slots the live tool reserves one round each for, counted back from the end — K + P + DST (Sleeper's league has no punter, hence only 2 there; this one's reserve is 3 of 20 rounds). Every K/P/DST is realistically still on the board that late. The live tool hides all three until then; type k, p or dst to override.

The value window is rounds 1–14, not 15+. Grouped by ADP-implied round, the best available POR stays clearly positive (18–163) through round 14, goes flat and noisy through the mid-teens, and sits at roughly 0–5 pts from round 18 on — right where K/P/DST unlock. A 10-team league's pool just drains slower per round than a 14-team one, which is why this window runs later than the Sleeper sheet's (rounds 6–9 there).
